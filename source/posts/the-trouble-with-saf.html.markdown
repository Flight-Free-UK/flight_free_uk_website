### S﻿AF (so-called 'sustainable' aviation fuel), is the aviation industry's preferred method of going green.

##### N﻿ew fuels can be used in the place of kerosene (conventional jet fuel), therefore reducing emissions without disrupting service or requiring a complete overhaul of aeroplanes or the industry.

**But what is SAF, and is it as green as it sounds?** 

So-called ‘sustainable’ aviation fuel, or SAF, is broadly anything that’s not fossil jet fuel (kerosene). This can be a biofuel, like plants, or animal fat, or used chip fat. Or it could be fossil-fuel derived, like landfill waste or used rubber tyres. Or it could be a synthetic fuel, made from electricity, like e-kerosene or liquid hydrogen.

The aviation industry [claims that it reduces emissions by 80%](https://www.airbus.com/en/innovation/energy-transition/our-commitment-to-saf), making it a sustainable alternative to conventional jet fuel.

You’ll notice the quotation marks around the word ‘sustainable’, and that we say ‘so-called’. We’re highly sceptical about any carbon savings that might be made from using SAF. 

Here’s why.

##### No reduction in emissions when burned

Regardless of how SAF is produced, it [creates just as much CO2 as kerosene when burned](https://www.iata.org/en/programs/sustainability/reports/saf-handbook/section-2.1). All the SAF pathways produce oil, just like any other oil, so there’s no reduction in the emissions that are created when the plane flies. 

![](/images/uploads/infographics.jpg)

Some sources of SAF can reduce some of the secondary warming impacts of aviation, like [reducing the formation of contrails](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-dlr-study-finds-sustainable-aviation-fuel-can-reduce-contrails/), which can help to reduce emissions. But litre-for-litre, SAF creates just as much CO2 as any other fuel.

The aviation industry knows this.

##### Savings are ‘up to’ 80% – but can be as low as 40%

The ‘savings’ come in the lifecycle of the fuel, which we will examine below. But it’s worth saying straight away that in order to be certified as SAF, [fuels only need to demonstrate a minimum 40% reduction in emissions](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/about-the-saf-mandate/the-saf-mandate-an-essential-guide) compared to kerosene. Yet they still quality under the umbrella of SAF, allowing airlines to claim an 80% reduction, regardless of what the saving actually is. 

##### Some sources of biofuels lead to deforestation

The most simple and easy-to-understand way that SAF reduces emissions is with biofuel crops. Plants absorb CO2 as they grow, so when they are harvested, turned into jet fuel and burned, the amount of CO2 that they’ve absorbed in their lifetime is balanced against the amount that is produced through the production and burning.

Calculations of carbon savings take into account [every step of the process](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9228054/), including land use change (changing from, say, agricultural crops to biofuel crops), harvesting, transportation, processing, storing, distributing, fuelling and burning. 

B﻿ut what happens when the land use change is not from previous agricultural land, but from biodiverse-rich natural landscapes, for example, rainforest? Biofuel cultivation is a leading driver of deforestation. Replacing natural eco-systems that support a variety of plant and animal life with an intensively-farmed monoculture is a disaster for the climate, making biofuels that are grown in this way far, far worse on balance than just using fossil fuels. 

##### All major SAF pathways are already used for something else

Most of the SAF we use in the UK comes from used cooking oil. The trouble is, we’re already using all of our used cooking oil for trucks and other vehicles on the road – actually, even [more than we currently have](https://www.transportenvironment.org/articles/europes-surging-demand-used-cooking-oil-could-fuel-deforestation). 

![](/images/uploads/mcdonalds-biodiesel.jpeg "All of our used cooking oil is currently used on the roads")

Another source is animal fat from the meat-processing industry, but we currently use all of that in pet food.

Diverting these sources to aviation means that the road fleet will have to use something else – potentially biofuels – meaning that overall, there’s no climate benefit. 

T﻿his principal applies across the board: every major source of SAF currently has a much more energy-efficient use than aviation, and therefore arguably a much better use.

##### Burning rubbish doesn’t qualify as sustainable

Some initiatives include turning [non-recyclable plastic into jet fuel](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cly0mln2g5po), or using [old tyres to create fuel](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c1jg56djd6jo). These are materials which would otherwise be incinerated or buried, and as the fossil fuel has already been extracted to make the plastic or rubber in the first place, the impact has arguably already happened.

But the process of creating jet-grade fuel is still energy- and carbon-intensive, and using these materials in this way gives no incentive to cut down on the amount of plastic we use or find better ways to reduce waste earlier in the supply chain. 

##### Scaling up is impossible

All waste-derived SAFs have a limited supply. Meeting the UK demand for fuel with biofuel crops would require [roughly half of all UK agricultural land](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2023/feb/28/scientists-uk-aviation-net-zero-ambitions-half-farmland-double-renewable-electricity). Commercial aviation consumes roughly 100 billion gallonsof jet fuel annually – replacing this with SAF would be impossible in terms of land space, resources and capacity. 

T﻿here's also the effect this has on communities. For example, scaling up biofuel for road transport has had a devastating effect in places like Malaysia, Indonesia and some countries in South America, leading not only to a degradation of land and decline in biodiversity but also humanitarian effects such as water shortages, rising food prices, and land conflicts.

##### The SAF market is open to fraud

T﻿he minute that something becomes sellable and desirable, someone will try to exploit it. SAF is now an in-demand product for airlines and companies wanting to show their customers they care about the climate, and carrying a premium price tag means it's a valuable commodity for traders.

In 2023 [it was reported](https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2023/dec/14/calls-for-tighter-rules-on-biofuels-imports-palm-oil-saf) that imported ‘used’ cooking oil was actually palm oil. 

##### Targets for increasing SAF use repeatedly missed

Between 2000 and 2020, [all but one of the industry’s 50 climate targets were either missed, revised down, or quietly forgotten about](https://www.wearepossible.org/our-reports/missed-target-a-brief-history-of-aviation-climate-targets). The industry used these targets to successfully lobby policymakers to allow its ongoing growth.

These included a target set in 2007 to reach 10% SAF use by 2017. In 2011, this was revised down to 6% by 2020, the following year reduced to 4.5%, then again to 3%, then to 2% by 2025. 

SAF use is currently 2% in the UK. The current target is for 10% SAF use by 2030, but some [analysis suggests that’s unlikely to happen](https://theicct.org/sites/default/files/publications/Sustainable-aviation-fuel-feedstock-eu-mar2021.pdf).

![](/images/uploads/possible-chart-saf.jpg "Chart from \"Missed Targets: A brief history of aviation climate targets\" report by Possible")

**Is there a genuinely zero-carbon fuel out there?**

T﻿he most promising alternatives to conventional jet fuel are [e-kerosene and liquid hydrogen](https://www.hy2gen.com/e-kerosene), as they are made  from water and air (both abundant resources) using electricity, so the production and combustion is zero-carbon as long as renewable energy is used.

T﻿he trouble is that creating liquid hydrocarbons is very energy intensive. [It would take 3-4 times the current global renewable energy generation](https://theicct.org/aviation-grid-jun22/) to produce synthetic e-fuels for aviation on current consumption trends.

T﻿his creates the same challenges as we see with electric flight: we need to consider if our limited renewable energy resources are best-used for an energy-intensive, arguably luxury, commodity. 

### SAF is a get-out-of-jail-free card – something that airlines can adopt without changing anything. But nothing changes in terms of emissions either. SAF really is greenwash.

### The best course of action would be to significantly cut how much we fly, and use the genuine solutions, like green technology, to decarbonise the flights that remain.
