### In October 2026, my husband and I travelled by rail from Glasgow to Sicily. 

##### Two weeks that bulged more than a Ryanair carry-on bag with delicious food, breathtaking views and multilayered culture. 

We crossed four countries in 15 days, without a plane or car involved – just trains and trainers.

We gave up flying a couple of years ago, persuaded by the example of our adult daughter, an environmental scientist and climate activist. Last year we took trains and a hire car trip through France to Spain, but this was our first go at flight- and car-free interrailing.

October is our preferred time for a holiday. My husband yearns for a last dose of sunshine before a season of damp, dark Glaswegian days. Sicily offered sun, scenery, history and character, food that turned out to be both delicious and excellent value and a chance to try out the lingo we'd been learning.

The outward journey was 3 days of train travel blended with comfy beds for the night, unique dining experiences and whistle stop tours of city highlights. We headed for France on the Eurostar, spent a night in Paris and carried on to elegant Bologna. Next day we reached Naples, with a few hours to explore before the 12-hour overnight ferry to Palermo in Sicily.

Truth be told, Naples and I didn't really hit it off. A few hours were enough to decide it wasn't for me.  I'm glad I’ve seen it and drawn that conclusion though. It’s a city with real character, but the noise and bustle, with people, cars and scooters coming at you from all directions, was a sensory overload. 

Sicily is a big island, so we split our time between Syracuse/Ortigia and Taormina, with day trips to bustling Palermo and Catania. Ortigia oozes history, charm and legend, and since the itinerary planning was mostly my husband's department, I'm claiming my one decision to make it a base. Taormina is designer label, not my thing, but its jaw-dropping views of cliffs, sea and Etna add some much-needed grit to the glamour. Catania, meanwhile, served up the best dish of pasta that has ever passed my lips. 

On the way home, we took the far shorter ferry from Messina to Villa San Giovanni in Calabria, then spent a few days in Rome, where we climbed enough steps and crossed enough squares to make up for time sat on trains. Our return route was via Lille rather than Paris so we travelled there through Switzerland, with a stopover in Basel. Switzerland couldn't match Italy for weather, food or value, but the mountain and valley views from an almost empty train graced with big, clean windows were stunning.

The time we spent on trains in Europe was the least memorable part of the experience, and I mean that as high praise. Apart from the two journeys between London and Glasgow, of which the least said the better, everything ran like clockwork. We made every connection, always had a seat and space for our luggage, and met no delays or cancellations. The toilets were clean and working, there was no anti-social behaviour and no turbulence!  Everywhere we disembarked, there was somewhere to leave our bags and someone to point the way.

The real challenge of the trip was the decision-making. Backpack or wheelie case? (We took one of each and I'm still undecided.) Opt for maximum freedom to choose where to stay and how long for, or book everything in advance for peace of mind?  Planning your own route brings so much choice and control that you have to embrace it or be overwhelmed. We did ok this time round but will be more confident in our decisions next time.

At times, the trip felt less like a holiday and more like Race Across the World crossed with a Duke of Edinburgh award. An achievement, a personal test and a lesson in teamwork.

For me, having studied languages at Uni and been lucky enough to live, study and work in several countries in my twenties, I have always felt more comfortable as a traveller than a tourist and this trip ticked that box for me. As an independent train traveller, you get to merge in with the locals on their way to work or college, to choose to eat, stay or shop off the main tourist strip. Student days are decades ago but we're definitely at a good life stage for this type of travel. Three quarrelling kids no longer in tow (although least there's no middle seat on a train) and still fit enough to run for a connection if we have to.

It wouldn’t be fair to say it was all sweetness and light – the jam-packed short trip across Paris was a little stressful and there were one or two days when I would have reduced the hours on the go. But it was a rich experience, and I would absolutely do it again, not just because it’s an adventure but because I'd like to do even better next time, perfect my planning and my travelling skills. A few tips:

* If you're thinking of trying it, plan together. I left the vast majority of the prep to my husband, and some of the many decisions needed would have benefited from a second opinion. I'd love to say more of this incredible experience was down to me. 
* Pack light, because when you’re place hopping, nobody sees you in the same dress twice.
* Aim for variety and contrast, like the noise of Naples followed by the charm of Ortigia. 
* Don't fret about your phone either: I lost mine early on, which was a worry, but there's not really time for one on a trip like this.

Wondering whether you could do it? You could, and the trains are the easy part.
