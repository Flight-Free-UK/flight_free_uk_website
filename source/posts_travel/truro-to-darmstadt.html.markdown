### My wife Kay has a fear of flying. 

##### So when we decided to visit Darmstadt, where our son Dexter’s girlfriend lives, we wanted to see if we could do it without getting on a plane. 

Darmstadt is approximately 20 minutes south of Frankfurt in Germany. 

Kay, after a major statistically unsurvivable RTC, needs to use a wheelchair and is also registered severely sight impaired, which isn’t anything to do with the RTC. Kay usually uses her powered chair but for this trip we used her manual chair which I pushed. 

We live in Cornwall, near to Truro. After looking at different viable ways to Darmstadt we decided on using the train all the way. Our trip was Truro to Paddington (GWR), Paddington to St Pancras, Eurostar to Brussels Midi, DeutscheBahn ICE service from Brussels to Frankfurt, and the final leg from Frankfurt to Darmstadt. All in all a trip of around 20 hours! 

Kay used the TrainPal app to book our tickets from Truro to London Paddington, then we had to contact GWR to arrange the assistance. This was pretty straightforward, and even though we upgraded to 1st class a few days before departure (using the app SeatFrog), GWR were very helpful and updated their records. Truro station has a little lounge area for 1st class passengers, and the customer service guy was very helpful and when it was time to get on the train he sorted the ramp for easy access. If you can stretch to or upgrade to 1st class it’s well worth it. With a wheelchair and luggage, the extra room does make a difference. 

We got to Paddington as scheduled at around midnight which meant a 20 minute taxi ride to St Pancras and as always the cabbie was great and dropped us as close as he could to the entrance.  

Access to the Eurostar desks starts at 05:00(ish) so we sat in St Pancras for over 4 hours through the night. Next time we’ll go up earlier and book a Premier Inn/Travelodge as waiting in St Pancras is brutal! There were many travellers doing the same but it felt a little intimidating at some points in the night. 

We booked the Eurostar well in advance which was easy on the phone. You get reduced prices for the disabled passenger and carer, and the wheelchair space is in Standard Plus so you basically get a free class upgrade. Check in is easy enough, with a separate desk for passengers who’ve booked assistance. 

We were then taken through to the baggage scan area which was the absolute worst part of the trip. I get that it was busy with all the early morning services leaving in quick succession but basically we were told to ‘push in’ the queues for the baggage scan machine, which attracted stares from the other passengers who'd queued up.

We had two suitcases and hand luggage, plus Kay’s rucksack of medication. I managed to ‘push in’ first and started loading the trays with our stuff, plus phones, coats etc. I went through the person scanner without a problem but Kay and Dex had to go through a couple of times, by which time our stuff was at the other end of the machine. I was trying to get it all off together, without any staff assistance, and in the meantime Kay had her medication rucksack still on the back of her wheelchair so that had to go through the baggage scan too. I thought I’d got everything and it wasn’t until we finally got to passport control that we realised the meds rucksack was still at the end of the scanner machine. 

We really could’ve done with assistance at that stage, an extra pair of hands and eyes as that situation could’ve easily ruined the holiday before we started if we’d forgot Kay’s meds. 

It wasn’t until we were actually on the Eurostar that we finally managed to relax and de-stress. The train staff were great, a ramp up into the Eurostar and reserved seats with a nice bit of room. 

We arrived at Brussels mid morning, where again the train staff were great, with the ramp ready and staff ready to guide us all the way along the platform, down in the lift and to the assistance office on the main concourse. Brussels Midi is extremely busy, with armed guards and sniffer dogs. The assistance staff were not shy in telling people to move to make room for the wheelchair! 

We had time between trains to sit and relax in the assistance office. I popped out for some fresh air and to get some Belgian Chocolates (there is a fantastic shop on the station concourse stuffed full of Belgian Chocolates, highly recommended!), and the station is very well equipped for toilet facilities, left luggage, shops, food outlets and of course a waffle shop or there is a waffle van outside. 

The staff were expecting us and took us up to the platform for the train to Frankfurt, again they didn’t mess about, people moved very quickly out of their way! 

Booking with Deutsche Bahn was a little tricky to get our heads around, being our first time, and using their website which has to be translated wasn’t the easiest thing. You [register with DB](https://www.deutschebahn.com/en), book AND reserve your seats, then you email them the details and they do the necessary arrangements and mail you back the amended booking details. They also have an English speaking phone line but it’s a German number which could work out expensive phoning from the UK. 

We were assisted onto the DB ICE service. It was a very busy train, a couple of guys were in our seats and again the train staff didn’t mess around, the guys were told to move for us, which they did. The seats were quite comfy with ample space for Kay’s chair and our luggage. Four and a half hours later we arrived in Frankfurt, where the train manager sorted the ramp and we were on the platform in no time. Frankfurt has an assistance team at the information kiosk who were aware of our arrival and took care of us. When the train arrived that was going to Darmstadt we were assisted on and 20 minutes later we arrived at Darmstadt Hbf, around 20 hours since we left Cornwall which wasn’t bad going for our first time attempting this! 

![](/images/uploads/kay-darmstadt_nkitto.jpeg "Kay at Darmstadt HB (station)")

We stayed at the InterCity hotel which was right next to the station and was perfect for our needs. The staff were very helpful, spoke good English, the room was clean and it’s around a mile and half from the city centre. 

The first thing we had to do was to get the German version of a RADAR key for the public toilets. It’s called the Euro Key, and if you order it online they say to allow a couple of weeks which was too late for us. Luckily for us the company that sends them is based in Darmstadt! So we went there first thing the next morning and purchased one for €30. You have to take proof of disability to [buy one](https://shop.cbf-da.de/product_info.php?products_id=61).

One tip: if you use the toilets that accept the EuroKey, and if someone is using the toilet, and they leave and hold the door open for you, do not go in! It throws out the mechanism and it doesn’t register someone is in there and won’t open when you want to leave. We found this out the hard way at Frankfurt station causing us a panic for a minute or two. I’m still not 100% sure how we got the door open again! 

Darmstadt is a lovely city. The LuisenPlatz is the main area for transport, pretty flat and cobbled which wasn't too much of a problem. We went up the Russian Church and the 'Wedding Building' (Mathildenhöhe). Most of our trip was spent in the city centre and the park nearby taking it all in as it was Kay's first time abroad. We had the most lovely weather too, just a brief shower on the Saturday morning, and the rest of the time was dry and clear. 

![](/images/uploads/darmstadt2_canva.jpg "Russian Orthodox Church in Darmstadt")

We used the regional train service to go back to Frankfurt for a day out later in the week. You have to book assistance with them by 20:00 the previous evening, and we booked this at Darmstadt Hbf ticket office the day before. Again, the customer service agent couldn’t have been more helpful, group ticket purchased for 4 of us for approx €38 and assistance sorted. 

Trams in Germany have dedicated carriages for wheelchair users indicated by a wheelchair sign, and a button to press on entry and exit to make the driver aware. We got on the tram going into Darmstadt one morning but at our stop on the Luisenplatz we pressed the button and the driver didn’t do anything and we couldn’t get off. Luckily the next stop was only 100m away and it had a raised platform for easy wheelchair exit/entry. I think on the display inside the tram, the stops with wheelchair friendly entry/exit have the standard wheelchair sign next to them. 

We found a lot of cafes etc had their toilets downstairs with no lifts which was a little annoying but the main shopping centre, the Luisen Centre, had public toilets and a very large accessible toilet. 

![](/images/uploads/kay-frankfurt_nkitto.jpeg "Day trip to Frankfurt")

The trip home was much the same as our outward journey with staff from all rail companies aware of us booking assistance. The baggage scan and passport control at Brussels was so much easier and less stressful than at St Pancras, because they had a dedicated baggage scanner machine for passengers who’d booked assistance on the Eurostar platform itself. The toughest part of the homeward journey was the midnight train from Paddington back to Truro. Paddington has a dedicated waiting room for passengers needing assistance which was very welcome, but we really wished we’d booked sleeper cabins as the seats were fairly uncomfortable and didn’t even recline a little. We left Darmstadt at 07:30 and arrived at Truro 07:30 the next morning. A long 25 hours to be honest. 

One thing to look into is if you need a Drs letter to carry medication into Europe/the EU. We had to get one with certain information that the German authorities needed to have, which was £50 and Kay was never asked to produce it once! Still, it is mandatory to produce it should it be asked for. 

Kay sorted this trip from start to finish, researched everything, booked it and dealt with GWR, Eurostar and DB with the queries we had. It was a little complicated and daunting but we know for next time what we need to do. 

GWR, Eurostar and DB were all very good (apart from the baggage scanning at St Pancras), and it all went very smoothly. At every station we got on or off at the relevant staff were aware of us and our needs. They were all very friendly and helpful here in the UK, Belgium and Germany. The ramps that were used to get us on and off the different trains, whether they were part of the train or stored at the relevant stations, were in good order, used correctly and Kay always felt safe using them. 

##### Travelling across Europe at 290km/h, then under the sea through the tunnel was a fantastic experience and we’d definitely travel that way again. We are already looking at going back in 2027!
