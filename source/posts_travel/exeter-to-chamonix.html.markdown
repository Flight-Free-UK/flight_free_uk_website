### A couple of years ago I bought a book called *Chamonix Hikes*.

##### I buy a lot of hiking guides but frequently they sit on the shelf and I rarely get past the ‘dreaming’ stage. 

B﻿ut I mentioned to my friend Mark that I’d read a great book describing dozens of hikes in the French Alps. He'd studied in nearby Chambery for a year and spent most of his time skiing in the Alps. How about a summer hike instead? He was up for it!

![](/images/uploads/chamonix-valley-sbarr.jpg "Chamonix in the valley")

Mark and I have travelled to France by train several times. Neither of us like flying and I no longer fly as a commitment to reducing my personal carbon emissions. As Mark hadn’t been to Europe for some years, I was tasked with organising the travel and accommodation. I love working out routes, itineraries and activities, so that was fine by me!

Our destination was Chamonix, a large resort town in the French Alps, nestled in the deep Chamonix Valley underneath Mont Blanc. Whilst I could be flexible with my timings, Mark had limited time off work, so we needed to be out and back in a long weekend: Thursday to Monday. Chamonix makes for a perfect long weekend trip because it’s simple and quick to reach without flying. 

I left Exeter late on the Wednesday afternoon and stopped over near St Pancras International in London. Mark was able to jump on an early train for the 25-minute trip from St Albans, so we met at 6:30am at the Eurostar terminal the next morning.

We were off on the 07:31 Eurostar to Paris and enjoyed blue sky views as we headed through Kent and then across northern France, arriving at Paris Gare du Nord at 11:00. Then we headed for the RER line D to make the short trip across to Gare de Lyon. The RER is a direct and quicker alternative to the metro in Paris as it uses fewer stops, but it’s effectively the same thing (an underground transit), taking just 7 minutes from Gare du Nord to Gare de Lyon. 

I already had two re-loadable Navigo cards for Paris public transport (usable on RER, metro and bus) but you can helpfully purchase these on Eurostar with a couple of pre-paid single trips. Then you just tap and go through the ticket gates. 

Arriving at Gare de Lyon is wonderful as you steadily ascend into Hall 2 of the main station via the travelator, emerging into a bright and busy concourse. Next stop Monop Daily for a lunch purchase of a cheese baguette, crisps and sweet things to snack on.

We caught the 12:18 TGV Lyria to Geneva, enjoying the lakes and mountains as we neared the French-Swiss border. Time for a coffee purchased from the lovely ‘Le Bar’ on board and it’s worth mentioning that all luggage carried on French trains must have a luggage label (see SNCF information on luggage [here](https://www.sncf-connect.com/en-en/help/transport-luggage)).

![](/images/uploads/chamonix-trains-sbarr.jpg "Arriving in the Alps")

Arriving at Geneva main station at 15:31 we quickly passed through French and Swiss customs (yes, this still happens, as Switzerland is not a member of the EU), then walked along Rue du Mont Blanc to the coach station for our connecting Flixbus to Chamonix Sud. 

We opted to take the coach for the final leg as it is quicker than taking the train. Now here is a tip – this is very much an outdoors coach station, with a small indoor waiting room. Although there is a departures board, you must be on your guard to watch which bay your coach will depart from, and you will need to wander round and check the destination and coach number on the front of the coaches. Ours was going all the way to Puglia!

And so we made our way to Chamonix, leaving Geneva on time at 16:20, via a fast motorway trip of 1 hour 10 minutes, with increasingly spectacular scenery. We arrived very close to the town’s centre, a five minute walk from our apartment. It was hot, beautiful and those mountains were calling! Plenty of time to check-in, do a small shop, have a tea (yes!) and find a nice place for a beer and tartiflette. 

If you have more time, you can reach Chamonix all the way by train, either by changing at Bellegarde or Geneva and taking a couple of local trains, the last of which is the wonderful narrow gauge Mont Blanc Express. The train option takes longer than the coach (another hour and a half), as there are several changes, although the route is very scenic.

And what’s in Chamonix when you arrive? If you’re a summer mountain adventurer, almost everything! The town is buzzing with people going hiking, white water rafting, paragliding, rock climbing or simply enjoying the views. And it’s just as buzzing in the winter, with easy access to the pistes. 

![](/images/uploads/chamonix-hike-sbarr.jpg)

If you stay in registered accommodation, you will get a Chamonix guest card, which includes free travel on the Mont Blanc Express up and down the valley, and there are various lift pass options for summer and winter that provide discounted or free public transport, see: [Home | Chamonix](https://en.chamonix.com/). I’ve worked out I could come back to Chamonix for many years and still find new hikes to try.

Mark and I had three wonderful days of hiking in beautiful sunny, warm weather. We did the Mer de Glace connoisseurs route on day 1, a very steep 1500 metres ascent from Chamonix to Le Brevent on day 2 and then a more leisurely lower level walk on day 3, taking in some of the many buvettes (mountain cafes) for some coffee and well-earned cake.

On the return trip, we re-trod our steps, and it was very easy for me to get all the way back to Exeter in a day. An 8:30 Blah Blah Bus from Chamonix got us to Geneva Airport (yes, you read that right!) for 10:10 and we then hopped on a train to Geneva main station to await our TGV Lyria. Rail engineering works meant the train took a diversion along a longer route, but it turned out to be a bonus: a wonderful route through the spectacular Jura mountains via Dijon to Paris. Great views and the extra hour slipped by. 

Our 18:02 Eurostar to London arrived at 19:30 then I took the 20:03 to Exeter, arriving at 22:30 (Mark got back to St Albans 20:15). The only issue on this return trip was that the RER line D in Paris was closed for engineering so we took the metro instead, which was too much like being in a sardine can for my linking, so I’d take a taxi next time.

##### Would I do it again? Yes, I’ve already booked for summer 2027 for another few days of glorious, easily accessible hiking in the Alps – all the way from Devon by train.
