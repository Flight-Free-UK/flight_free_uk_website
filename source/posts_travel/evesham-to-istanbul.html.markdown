### What is it that makes you take a major life changing decision?  Is it a gradual build-up of things or just one catalyst? 

##### For me it could have been watching the 2009 Age of Stupid for the tenth time, or the Stern report which said that ‘climate change is the greatest market failure in human history because the price of carbon emissions does not reflect the environmental damage they cause.’

Are we the cause of climate change or are we being prevented from reversing it by vested interests? I feel that it is the latter. The fact that aviation fuel remains untaxed and emissions from flying are often greenwashed gives me eco-rage.

Rather than succumb to the rage we chose to do something about it and my wife Becky and I stopped flying about ten years ago. We came up with the idea of travelling overland to Australia to watch the British Lions tour in 2025 – but these (modest!) plans were put on hold as a consequence of the war in Ukraine.

So we decided to see as much of Europe as we could by train. A trip to Florence and Tuscany was followed by a tour of Scandinavia, then a journey down to Andalusia and across the Straits of Gibraltar to Morocco. Each trip convinced us that more was possible and in 2025 we decided we’d like to reach the boundary between Europe and Asia.

![](/images/uploads/andrew-journey.jpg)

We like circular routes so with the help of a good paper map and the Interrail planner app, we planned a route down through eastern Europe and back up through Greece and Italy: through London, Brussels, Munich, Vienna, Budapest, Brasov and Bucharest to Istanbul, then back through Sofia, Thessalonika, Igoumenitsa, Brindisi, Rome, Milan, Paris, London and home.

The whole trip was such a romantic and exciting experience. We took three sleeper trains which were all incredibly comfortable with amazing staff who really look after you. There were also two coaches, from Sofia (Bulgaria) to Thessalonika (Greece) and from Thessalonika to Igoumenitsa. The contrast between countries makes it exciting, so we didn’t ever get tired of the travel. There is an amazing diversity of scenery across Europe and we also had a huge range of temperature from -9C in Brasov (Romania) to mid twenties in Paris, which makes packing quite challenging!

Despite the number of trains and connections we only had one delay and that was from Brussels to Frankfurt. There was also a slight worry because you can’t book the sleeper train to Istanbul until after you’ve left Bucharest, so we hoped that what [The Man in Seat61](https://www.seat61.com/Turkey.htm) said was true and that we’d be able to purchase tickets when we got to Ruse, just inside the Bulgarian border*. It wasn’t an issue and allowed us to engage with some charming booking staff. 

*\*﻿you can now book tickets in advance. See the [Türkiye page on Seat61.com](https://www.seat61.com/Turkey.htm) for the step-by-step instructions!*

But when joining the sleeper train in Dimitrovgrad at close to midnight we realised that we hadn’t eaten and had nothing to take on the train. We identified a pizza place reasonably close to the station and hoped that it was open, which, fortunately, it was. It was a slightly surreal experience: most of the customers were in 1980s knitwear, had mullets (male and female) and were smoking inside. Despite our complete lack of Bulgarian, we were able to procure a pizza and a bottle of wine for the journey.

Trying to pick a favourite part sparks a conversation between us that can last for hours as we dissect the journey. We love to visit food halls or markets which give you a flavour of the country in more ways than one. We like to try a cake in each country: Austria has the Sashatorte, Hungary has the Dobos Torte. In Romania every station has a station master with a very large hat that waves off every train. We expected Istanbul to be similar to Marrakech but it was far more European that we had imagined. 

When we were in Munich, we visited the bierkeller where Hitler started the National Socialist party, and the implications of that really hit home when we undertook a walking tour of the communist era in Sofia. We try to do walking tours led by locals in each place, which give you insights that you may not get from guide books. In every country without exception people were incredibly friendly. Some were surprised by the nature of our journey but very supportive of our principles.

After five weeks of travel, crossing the Adriatic felt like we were on the home stretch.  We had a couple of nights in Brindisi and a few more in Rome and then a single night in Milan. It was fashion week in Milan so the hotels had all hiked their prices and every black Mercedes in Europe seemed to be in town. It was a memorable experience.

Our journeys have given us so many incredibly memorable experiences in places that we would otherwise have just flown over. And we have only just scratched the surface. There are the Baltic states, Eastern Germany and Poland, northern Spain and Portugal and it seems that the rail industry is starting to respond to this potential market with more sleeper services being introduced.

Over time we have refined our luggage and now we each have an airline carry on compatible case and a small rucksack with food and entertainment for the journey, which can be used as a day bag in each destination. We have used [Interrail](https://www.interrail.com/en) tickets for all of our journeys and have found them relatively inexpensive and flexible. The app which accompanies the pass is also really useful and easy to navigate. For accommodation we have used Airbnb which again is flexible and allows you to shop for local produce to cook at the accommodation.

##### It is a remarkable feeling to leave our house in Worcestershire and end up in an exotic destination without flying and we would definitely recommend it as an alternative.
